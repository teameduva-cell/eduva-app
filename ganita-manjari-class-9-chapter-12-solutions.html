📖 Chapter Summary

Quadrilateral = 4 sides wali closed figure. Is chapter mein hum inke **angles ka sum (360°)**, **parallelogram ke properties**, aur **special quadrilaterals** (rectangle, rhombus, square, trapezium) ki family samajhte hain — triangles ke baad geometry ka sabse important hissa!

🎯 Key Concepts

  • Quadrilateral ke 4 angles ka yog hamesha 360°
  • Parallelogram: opposite sides barabar aur parallel • opposite angles barabar • diagonals ek doosre ko bisect karti hain
  • Rectangle = parallelogram + 90° angles • Rhombus = parallelogram + sab sides barabar (diagonals ⊥)
  • Square = rectangle + rhombus (dono ki best qualities!)
  • Trapezium = sirf ek pair parallel sides
  • Midpoint theorem: triangle ke do sides ke midpoints milane wali line teesri side ke parallel aur aadhi hoti hai

✍️ Original Solved Questions

Q1. Quadrilateral ke teen angles 80°, 100°, 70° hain — chautha angle?
Solution: Yog = 360° → 4th = 360 − (80+100+70) = 360 − 250 = 110° ✅
Q2. Parallelogram ABCD mein ∠A = 70° — baaki angles nikalo.
Solution: Opposite angles barabar: ∠C = 70°. Adjacent angles 180° ke: ∠B = ∠D = 180−70 = 110° ✅
Q3. Triangle ABC mein D aur E, AB aur AC ke midpoints hain. Agar DE = 4 cm, toh BC = ?
Solution: Midpoint theorem: DE = ½ BC → BC = 2×4 = 8 cm ✅
Q4. True ya False: (a) Square is a rectangle (b) Rectangle is a square (c) Diagonals of rhombus are perpendicular.
Solution: (a) True ✅ (special rectangle) (b) False ✅ (har rectangle square nahi) (c) True ✅
Q5. Ek quadrilateral ke diagonals ek doosre ko bisect karti hain aur equal hain — ye kya ho sakta hai? Aur agar perpendicular bhi hon?
Solution: Bisect + equal → rectangle ✅ • Agar perpendicular bhi hon → square ✅ (tests wala favorite question!)

📘 Notes in English

  • Quadrilateral: Closed figure with 4 sides. Angle sum = 360° always.
  • Parallelogram: Opposite sides parallel & equal • opposite angles equal • adjacent angles 180° • diagonals BISECT each other.
  • Rectangle: Parallelogram + all angles 90° → diagonals EQUAL too.
  • Rhombus: Parallelogram + all sides equal → diagonals PERPENDICULAR, and they bisect the angles.
  • Square: Rectangle + Rhombus — everything equal, everything 90°. King of the family!
  • Trapezium: Only ONE pair of parallel sides.
  • Kite: Two pairs of adjacent equal sides; one diagonal perpendicularly bisects the other.
  • Midpoint Theorem: Segment joining midpoints of two sides of a triangle is PARALLEL to the third side and HALF of it (DE = ½ BC).
  • Quick test: Diagonals bisect? → parallelogram • +Equal? → rectangle • +Perpendicular? → rhombus • +both? → square.

📕 हिंदी में नोट्स

  • चतुर्भुज: 4 भुजाओं वाली बंद आकृति। कोणों का योग हमेशा 360°।
  • समांतर चतुर्भुज: सम्मुख भुजाएँ समानांतर और बराबर • सम्मुख कोण बराबर • आसन्न कोण 180° • विकर्ण एक-दूसरे को द्विभाजित करते हैं।
  • आयत: समांतर चतुर्भुज + सभी कोण 90° → विकर्ण भी बराबर।
  • समचतुर्भुज (Rhombus): समांतर चतुर्भुज + सभी भुजाएँ बराबर → विकर्ण लंबवत और कोण भी द्विभाजित करते हैं।
  • वर्ग: आयत + समचतुर्भुज — सब बराबर, सब 90°। पूरे परिवार का बादशाह!
  • समलंब (Trapezium): सिर्फ एक जोड़ी समानांतर भुजाएँ।
  • पतंग (Kite): आसन्न भुजाओं के दो जोड़े बराबर; एक विकर्ण दूसरे का लंबवत द्विभाजक।
  • मध्य-बिंदु प्रमेय: त्रिभुज की दो भुजाओं के मध्य-बिंदुओं को मिलाने वाला रेखाखंड तीसरी भुजा के समानांतर और आधा (DE = ½ BC)।
  • झटपट पहचान: विकर्ण द्विभाजित? → समांतर • +बराबर? → आयत • +लंबवत? → समचतुर्भुज • +दोनों? → वर्ग ✅

➕ Extra Practice — same level (original)

➕ Quadrilateral ke angles x, 2x, 3x, 4x mein hain — har angle nikalo.
Answer: x + 2x + 3x + 4x = 360 → 10x = 360 → x = 36° → Angles: 36°, 72°, 108°, 144° ✅
➕ Rhombus ke diagonals 10 cm aur 24 cm hain — side nikalo.
Answer: Diagonals bisect + perpendicular → half-diagonals 5, 12 → side = √(25+144) = 13 cm ✅
➕ True ya False: 'Har rhombus parallelogram hota hai, par har parallelogram rhombus nahi.'
Answer: True ✅ — rhombus par parallelogram की सारी properties हैं (special case), पर parallelogram की sides बराबर होना ज़रूरी नहीं।

📗 Exercise Set 12.1 — Quadrilateral Basics (5 Qs)

Q1. ABCD mein: (i) AB ke adjacent/opposite sides (ii) ∠A ke adjacent/opposite angles (iii) vertex names ke bina definitions?
(i) AB ke adjacent: BC aur AD • opposite: CD ✅
(ii) ∠A ke adjacent: ∠B, ∠D • opposite: ∠C ✅
(iii) Opposite sides = jo share nahi karte (non-adjacent); opposite angles = jo share nahi karte vertex ✅
⭐Q2. Internal angle ki precise definition (non-convex ke liye bhi)?
Vertex A par internal angle = wo angle jo quadrilateral ke andar banta hai — specifically, do adjacent sides (jaise AB, AD) ke beech ka wo angle jo opposite vertex C ke taraf khulta hai. Non-convex mein ye >180° ho sakta hai (reflex) — opposite vertex use karne se definition dono cases mein kaam karti hai ✅
Q3. AB ∥ DC — ABCD non-convex ho sakta? AB = CD ho toh? ∠A = ∠C ho toh?
(i) Ha ✅ — AB ∥ DC ke saath bhi non-convex trapezium ban sakta hai (D ko triangle ke andar fold karke). Parallelism convexity guarantee nahi deta.
(ii) AB = CD se bhi non-convex ho sakta — equal sides ka matlab convex nahi.
(iii) ∠A = ∠C bhi guarantee nahi — but agar dono pairs opposite angles barabar hon (parallelogram condition) tab convex zaroori hai ✅
Q4. Teen non-collinear points A,B,C — lines AB,BC,CA plane ko 7 regions mein baantti hain. D har region mein: ABCD convex, non-convex, ya self-intersecting?
7 regions: 1 = triangle ke andar → non-convex (D andar, ek reflex angle) • 3 = teeno sides ke "aage" wale unbounded regions → convex • 3 = teeno vertices ke paas wale (extensions ke beech) → self-intersecting (bowtie) ✅
Q5. Kya quadrilateral self-intersecting aur non-planar dono ho sakta?
Ha ✅ — 3D mein bhi do non-adjacent sides intersect kar sakte hain (jaise 3D bowtie). Planar hone ki zaroorat nahi self-intersection ke liye.

📗 Exercise Set 12.2 — Parallelogram Properties (4 Qs)

Q1. True/False: (i) right angle wala parallelogram = rectangle (ii) perpendicular diagonals wala rhombus = square (iii) equal diagonals wala parallelogram = rectangle?
(i) TRUE ✅ (parallelogram mein ek angle 90° → sab 90°)
(ii) FALSE ❌ — rhombus ke diagonals hamesha perpendicular hote hain! Square tabhi jab angles bhi 90° hon.
(iii) TRUE ✅ (equal diagonals + bisect ⇒ rectangle)
Q2. Parallelogram ABCD mein diagonal AC, ∠A ko bisect karta hai — ∠C bhi bisect karega aur ABCD rhombus hai, dikhao?
ΔABC ≅ ΔCDA (SSS: AB=CD, BC=DA, AC common) → ∠BAC = ∠DCA, ∠BCA = ∠DAC. Given ∠BAC = ∠DAC, so ∠BCA = ∠DCA → AC bisects ∠C ✅. Ab ∠BAC = ∠BCA (dono = ∠DAC = ∠DCA... wait: ∠BAC = ∠DAC given; ∠DAC = ∠BCA alternate) → ∠BAC = ∠BCA → AB = BC (sides opposite equal angles) → rhombus ✅
Q3. Converses — Yes/No + extra condition: (i) diagonals bisect angles ⇒ rhombus? (ii) diagonals perpendicular bisect ⇒ rhombus? (iii) equal diagonals ⇒ rectangle?
(i) YES ✅ (already proved in Q2 style)
(ii) YES ✅ — perpendicular + bisect ⇒ all sides equal (half-diagonals as legs)
(iii) NO ❌ — isosceles trapezium ke bhi equal diagonals hote hain! Extra condition: diagonals bisect each other bhi hon (tab rectangle) ✅
Q4. ⭐ ABCD parallelogram (AB ≠ BC) — chaaron angle bisectors ke pairwise intersections P,Q,R,S rectangle form karte hain. AB = BC assume kyun nahi kiya?
Kyunki agar AB = BC ho (rhombus), toh angle bisectors diagonals ban jaate hain aur sab ek hi point (centre) pe milte hain — P,Q,R,S ban hi nahi paate! Isliye unequal sides zaroori hain taaki bisectors ek chhota sa rectangle ban sakein ✅ (adjacent angles 180° ke hain, half-angles 90° milake rectangle ke angles banate hain)

📗 Exercise Set 12.3 — Midpoint Theorem & Varignon (5 Qs)

Q1. P,Q,R midpoints of AB,AC,BC — (i) ΔPQR congruent to ΔQPA aur do aur triangles? (ii) sirf ΔPQR se poora ΔABC reconstruct?
(i) Midpoint theorem se PQ = ½BC = BR = RC, QR = ½AB = AP = PB, PR = ½AC = AQ = QC. So ΔPQR ≅ ΔQPA (SSS) ≅ ΔRBP ≅ ΔQRC ✅
(ii) Ha ✅ — ΔPQR ke har side ko double extend karo; teeno sides par jaake A, B, C mil jaate hain (half-scale se original)
Q2. ΔABC mein M,N midpoints of AB,AC — D on BC — MN, AD ko bisect karta hai?
Let MN meet AD at O. MN ∥ BC (midpoint theorem). In ΔABD: M midpoint of AB, MO ∥ BD → O midpoint of AD (converse of midpoint theorem) ✅
Q3. ⭐ Trapezium ABCD (AB ∥ DC, AB ≠ CD), G,H midpoints of AC,BD — GH ∥ AB. AB ≠ CD kyun?
Proof: ΔACD mein G midpoint of AC; GH part of line through midpoints... Join AH to CD etc. Standard result: midpoints of diagonals of trapezium ka join parallel to bases, length = |AB−CD|/2. AB ≠ CD ✅ — agar barabar hote toh ABCD parallelogram ban jaata (equal + parallel ⇒ parallelogram) aur "diagonals ke midpoints" coincide kar jaate — GH zero length ka point ban jaata, line nahi!
Q4. Quadrilateral ABCD ke sides ke midpoints P,Q,R,S — (i) PR aur QS bisect each other (ii) AC = BD ho toh PR ⊥ QS — converse?
(i) Varignon parallelogram: PQ ∥ AC ∥ SR, QR ∥ BD ∥ PS → PQRS parallelogram → diagonals PR, QS bisect ✅
(ii) AC = BD ⇒ PQ = QR (sab halves barabar) ⇒ rhombus ⇒ diagonals perpendicular ✅ Converse: PR ⊥ QS ⇒ rhombus ⇒ PQ = QR ⇒ AC = BD — converse TRUE ✅
Q5. ⭐ Varignon parallelogram PQRS se ABCD reconstruct — (iii) PQRS square ⇒ AC ⊥ BD aur AC = BD — converse?
(i)-(ii) Extend karo: S ko A-side aur D-side double distance pe A',D' rakho; P,Q,R similar — congruence se A'B'C'D' ≅ ABCD ✅
(iii) PQRS square ⇒ sides equal + angles 90° ⇒ AC = BD (equal halves) aur PQ ⊥ QR ⇒ AC ⊥ BD ✅ Converse: AC ⊥ BD + AC = BD ⇒ PQ = QR + PQ ⊥ QR ⇒ square — TRUE ✅

📗 Exercise Set 12.4 — Tiling (2 Qs)

Q1. Regular pentagon se plane tile kyun nahi hota?
Regular pentagon ka interior angle = 108°. Tiling ke liye vertex par angles ka sum 360° hona chahiye. 108° se 360 nahi banta (360/108 = 3.33...). Isliye gaps ya overlaps — regular pentagon se tiling impossible ✅ (irregular pentagon se possible — 2015 discovery!)
Q2. Non-convex 4-gon "DART" se plane tile karo — dono methods?
Method 1: DART ko do congruent triangles mein kaato (diagonal se), triangles se tile karo, phir wapas join — har 2 triangles = 1 DART ✅
Method 2: 180° rotation se DART ke copies place karo (head-to-tail) — rows ban jaati hain ✅ Preference apni!

📗 End-of-Chapter Exercises — Chapter 12 (24 Qs)

Q1. Parallelogram fact se triangle se plane tiling?
Har triangle ko apne midpoint triangle ke saath milke ek parallelogram banao (rotate 180° about midpoint) — parallelograms se plane tile hota hai → triangles se bhi ✅
Q2. Equally spaced horizontal lines pe ek slant line ka midpoint mark karo?
Slant line ke do endpoints 7th aur 3rd line pe hain — midpoint unke average pe: (7+3)/2 = 5th line. Tick marks se: wo point jahan line 5th horizontal ko kaati hai ✅ (equal spacing = linear interpolation)
Q3. ⭐ Self-intersecting ABCD (AB, CD intersect at E) — ∠A+∠B+∠C+∠D < 360°? Kya = 2° ho sakta?
Bowtie mein: ∠A + ∠D = ∠BEC (exterior angle of ΔAED) aur ∠B + ∠C = ∠AED. Total = ∠BEC + ∠AED = 2(∠AED) < 360° (kyunki ∠AED < 180°) ✅ 2° banane ke liye ∠AED ≈ 1° rakho — practically impossible par theoretically possible (E bahut door ho) ✅
Q4. Parallelogram ABCD, P,Q on BD with DP = BQ — APCQ parallelogram?
Diagonals of ABCD bisect: AO = OC, BO = OD. DP = BQ ⇒ PO = QO (subtract from halves). APCQ ke diagonals AC aur PQ bisect each other at O ⇒ APCQ parallelogram ✅
Q5. Right triangle paper fold — A touches B — crease se AB aur AC dono ke midpoint?
Crease = perpendicular bisector of AB (fold property) ⇒ M (midpoint of AB) crease pe hai. Right angle at B? Triangle ABC right at B: crease (perp. bisector of AB) ∥ BC (kyunki BC ⊥ AB) — through midpoint of AB, parallel to BC ⇒ meets AC at its midpoint (converse midpoint theorem) ✅
Q6. ⭐ Triangle ko 3 congruent triangles mein kaato (Lee Sallows!) — (iii) 2 congruent triangles?
(i) Medians se 6 triangles; ΔMPB + ΔMPC ko BC pe align karo (B↔C) → 1 triangle; similarly baaki — 3 congruent triangles ban jaate hain, sides = original sides ke 2/3 pattern ✅
(ii) Repeat → 9 triangles with sides AB/3, BC/3, AC/3 ✅
(iii) 2 congruent: median AD se ΔABD, ΔACD — congruent only if AB = AC... general case: nahi ho sakta with straight cuts (area argument) ✅
Q7. ⭐ Trapezium midpoint theorem: E midpoint of AD, EF ∥ AB ⇒ F midpoint of BC, EF = (AB+CD)/2 — converse?
(i) Join BD, let EF meet BD at M. ΔABD: M midpoint of BD (EM ∥ AB). ΔBDC: M midpoint of BD, MF ∥ DC ⇒ F midpoint of BC. EF = EM + MF = AB/2 + CD/2 ✅
(ii) Converse: F midpoint of BC ⇒ same construction se EF ∥ AB ✅ (dono proofs valid)
Q8. Parallelogram diagonals intersect O — line through O meets AB, CD at P,Q — O midpoint of PQ?
ΔAOP ≅ ΔCOQ (AO = OC, ∠OAP = ∠OCQ alternate, ∠AOP = ∠COQ vertical) ⇒ OP = OQ ✅ (ASA congruence)
⭐Q9. Trapezium AD=3, BC=5 — E,F midpoints of non-parallel sides — areas ratio AEFD : EBCF?
EF = (3+5)/2 = 4 (Q7 result). AEFD: parallel sides 3,4; EBCF: 4,5. Same height (distance between parallels same). Areas ratio = (3+4) : (4+5) = 7 : 9 ✅
Q10. 4 points (no 3 collinear) — kitne quadrilaterals? kitne self-intersecting/convex?
(i) 4! orderings = 24, par har quadrilateral 4 rotations × 2 directions se count → 24/8 = 3 distinct quadrilaterals ✅
(ii) Agar 1 point triangle ke andar hai: 1 convex + 1 non-convex + 1 self-intersecting... generally: 3 mein se — convex 1 (cyclic order), baaki 2 mein se 1 self-intersecting 1 non-convex (case-dependent). Classic answer: 1 convex, 1 non-convex, 1 self-intersecting ✅
Q11. PQ ∥ BC, AP=1, AQ=√5: (i) PB=2 → QC? (ii) PB=1/3 → QC? (iii) PB=2/3? (iv) AP/PB rational ⇒ AP/PB = AQ/QC?
Dotted lines se similar triangles: AP/AB = AQ/AC ⇒ 1/(1+PB) = √5/(√5+QC)
(i) 1/3 = √5/(√5+QC) ⇒ √5+QC = 3√5 ⇒ QC = 2√5 ✅
(ii) 1/(4/3) = 3/4 = √5/(√5+QC) ⇒ QC = (4/3 − 1)√5 = √5/3 ✅
(iii) 1/(5/3) = 3/5 ⇒ QC = 2√5/3 ✅
(iv) Rational case mein QC = √5 × (PB/AP) — ye equality hold karti hai (Thales/Basic proportionality) ✅
Q12. ⭐ Parallelogram ABCD — M,N midpoints of AB, CD — DM, BN trisect AC — aur PQ ko trisect karne ke 3 tareeke?
AMND parallelogram: DM, AN diagonals bisect... AC ke points: trisection at 1/3, 2/3. DM aur BN dono AC ko wahin kaat-te hain (midpoint theorem chain) ✅
PQ trisect karne ke tareeke: (1) Ye parallelogram method (2) Q11 wala parallel-lines method (3) Centroid theorem se (triangle banao, medians) ✅
Q13. Varignon theorem non-convex/self-intersecting mein? Exception?
(i) Ha — midpoint connector hamesha parallelogram banata hai (vector proof: P+Q = R+S regardless of convexity) ✅
(ii) Exception: jab diagonals ka midpoint same ho (ABCD parallelogram hi ho!) — tab PQRS ek point ban jaata hai ✅
(iii) Non-planar mein bhi vector proof kaam karta — theorem TRUE in 3D ✅
Q14. P,Q,R,S on sides, PQRS parallelogram — kya P,Q,R,S midpoints hone zaroori?
Nahi ✅ — infinite positions possible (jaise trapezium mein sides ke any equal-fraction points). Midpoint theorem ka converse "if parallelogram then midpoints" false hai. (Varignon ke liye zaroori, general ke liye nahi)
Q15. ⭐ Midpoint theorem proof complete karo (extend PQ to S) — converse bhi?
Extend PQ to S so that PQ = QS. ΔAPQ ≅ ΔCSQ (SAS: AQ=QC, PQ=QS, vertical angles) → AP = CS, ∠APQ = ∠CSQ ⇒ AB ∥ CS... APCB parallelogram ⇒ PS ∥ BC ⇒ PQ ∥ BC, PQ = ½PS = ½BC ✅ Converse: same S construction se reverse karke P midpoint ⇒ Q midpoint ✅
⭐Q16-18. Rhombus/rectangle converses; non-planar angle sum < 360°; tiling by translation — key results?
Q16: Har property ka converse formulate karo — kuch true (diagonals ⊥ bisect ⇒ rhombus), kuch false (equal diagonals ⇒ rectangle needs bisect condition) ✅
Q17: Non-planar quadrilateral = do triangles "hinge" se jude — angle sum < 360° (folding se angles ghatta hai). 2° possible jab hinge almost flat (180° fold) ✅
Q18: Translation tiling — copy slide karo; parallelograms S'OME', E'O'M'S etc. se gap-fill — proof via parallelogram congruence ✅
Q19. 4-gon with sides 2, 5, 11 — fourth side 100? 10? 1? Possible range?
Triangle inequality on "two triangles": split into two triangles by a diagonal. General rule: largest side < sum of other three (strict). Sorted a≤b≤c≤d: need a+b+c > d.
2+5+11 = 18 → d < 18. So 100 ❌, 10 ✅ (2+5+11>10), 1 ❌ (then sorted 1,2,5,11: 1+2+5=8 < 11 ❌). Range: 11−(2+5) < d < 18 → 4 < d < 18 ✅
Q20. n-gon ke diagonals ki sankhya?
Har vertex se (n−3) diagonals (khud + 2 adjacent chhod ke). Total = n(n−3)/2 (double-count divide by 2). 3→0, 4→2, 5→5, 6→9, 7→14, 8→20. New vertex add karne par (n−2) diagonals add hote hain ✅ Formula: n(n−3)/2 ✅
Q21. n-gon ke angles ka sum?
(n−2) triangles mein kaato (ek vertex se diagonals) → sum = (n−2)×180° ✅ (3→180, 4→360, 5→540, 6→720...)
Q22. Midpoint theorem ke multiple converses — (P MID)(Q MID)(PRLL)(HALF) combinations?
4 conditions: original = (P MID)(Q MID)⇒(PRLL)(HALF). Possible converses: (PRLL)(HALF)⇒(P MID)(Q MID) — TRUE (Q15 proof reversed). (P MID)(PRLL)⇒(Q MID) = Theorem 7 ✅. (P MID)(HALF)⇒? — ye wala case examin karna hai: agar P midpoint hai aur PQ = ½BC, kya PQ ∥ BC? Ha (similar triangles se) — par Q midpoint hona zaroori nahi! (counterexample: Q any point with right distance on a circle arc) — isliye Theorem 7 (parallel wala) sabse useful hai ✅
⭐Q23. Teenon tiling methods ki validity proof?
Har method mein: copies ke beech gaps nahi (construction se adjacency guaranteed) + overlaps nahi (congruent copies, boundary matching) + plane cover (translation/rotation group se infinite grid). Formal proof: each method generates a wallpaper group; fundamental domain = 4-gon; group action transitive on plane ✅
⭐Q24. Square mein shaded fraction kitna? (Fig 12.43 — 4 triangles at corners, tilted inner square)
Classic puzzle: outer square side 3 units (tick marks), corner triangles legs 1,2 → inner square side = √(1²+2²) = √5. Shaded = inner square = 5/9 ✅ (area ratio = 5/9 of total). (Har corner triangle area 1, total corners 4, inner 5, outer 9)

❓ FAQs

Kaunsa quadrilateral kaunsa hota hai — kaise yaad karein?

Family tree yaad karo: Quadrilateral → Parallelogram → (Rectangle / Rhombus) → Square. Har baap ki property bacchon mein bhi hoti hai.

Diagonals wale questions ka shortcut?

Bisect kare? (parallelogram) + Equal? (rectangle) + Perpendicular? (rhombus) + Sab? (square) — ye 3 tests se 90% questions ho jaate hain.

Exam mein kitne marks ka aata hai?

Geometry proof-based aata hai — angle nikalna, prove karna, construction — practice zaroori. In properties ko thm chhote cards mein likh ke revise karo!
